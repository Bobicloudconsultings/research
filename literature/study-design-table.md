# SENSS Proposal Redesign — Study 1–4 Design Table

## Purpose

This table develops the detailed design of the four studies before the proposal text is rewritten.

The purpose is to test the revised study architecture against the main issues identified in the SENSS thematic review, particularly:

- project feasibility and scope;
- clarity of the research questions;
- realistic participant recruitment and access;
- meaningful integration of different disciplinary perspectives;
- ethical considerations when researching violence and vulnerable populations;
- clear connections between the four studies.

The redesign follows the agreed principle:

> **Keep → Refine → Replace only where necessary.**

The original research problem and overall focus on justice responses to cult and gang violence (CGV) in Southern Nigeria are retained. Studies 1 and 2 are substantially redesigned, while the core logic of Studies 3 and 4 is retained and strengthened.

## Emerging Study Logic

The revised studies are intended to form a connected programme rather than four separate projects:

**Study 1 → Why people prefer particular forms of justice**

↓  

**Study 2 → How justice, repair and reintegration are understood by people involved in offending or violence**

↓  

**Study 3 → How these issues operate within communities and community-based responses**

↓  

**Study 4 → Which justice needs people prioritise when judging whether justice has been achieved**

This sequence allows the later studies to build on evidence generated by the earlier studies.

## Interdisciplinary Contribution

The disciplines should not simply be listed as part of the proposal. Each should make a distinct contribution to the research.

### Evolutionary Psychology

Evolutionary psychology provides the framework for examining:

- punishment;
- deterrence;
- revenge;
- compensation;
- repair;
- reconciliation;
- responses to wrongdoing;
- the conditions under which people support different forms of justice.

### Forensic Psychology

Forensic psychology contributes to understanding:

- violence and offending;
- pathways into and out of offending;
- disengagement from violence;
- reintegration;
- offender perspectives;
- risk and risk-related considerations;
- research involving sensitive and potentially vulnerable populations;
- ethical challenges associated with researching offending and violence.

### Community and Peacebuilding Perspectives

The community and peacebuilding perspective contributes to understanding:

- local responses to violence;
- community justice;
- social norms;
- community dialogue;
- traditional and community institutions;
- trust;
- reconciliation;
- social relationships;
- social cohesion;
- how justice interventions operate within real community settings.

### Integration

Together, these perspectives allow the research to examine justice at several connected levels:

**individual psychological motivations → offending and reintegration → community responses → wider judgements about justice**

The aim is therefore not to add disciplines to the project, but to show how each discipline helps answer a different part of the overall research problem.

## Key Feasibility Questions

Before rewriting the proposal, each study should be tested against the following questions:

### 1. Recruitment

- Who exactly will participate?
- How will participants be identified and recruited?
- Is access already available through the proposed partner organisations?
- Would recruitment require additional organisations or permissions?
- Can the proposed sample realistically be obtained within the doctoral timeframe?

### 2. Geography

- How many Nigerian locations are genuinely necessary?
- Can the research be conducted across all five original states?
- Would reducing the geographical scope strengthen feasibility without undermining the research question?
- Can comparisons between communities with different levels of CGV realistically be made?

### 3. Participant Groups

- Which participant groups are essential?
- Which groups would add useful information but make the project unnecessarily complex?
- Is direct access to offenders realistic and ethically acceptable?
- What is the specific justification for including community leaders?
- How will the voices of directly affected community members be represented?

### 4. Ethics and Safety

- Could participation cause distress or retraumatisation?
- What information about previous violence needs to be collected?
- Can the research questions be answered without requiring unnecessary disclosure of traumatic experiences?
- How will confidentiality be protected?
- What safeguards are needed when working with former/current offenders?
- What risks could the research create for the researcher or participants?
- How will researcher positionality and reflexivity be addressed?

### 5. Methodological Complexity

- Is each method necessary to answer the research question?
- Can each study realistically be completed within its allocated period?
- Does each study produce evidence that is genuinely needed by the next study?
- Are there methods that could be simplified without weakening the research?

### 6. Interdisciplinary Integration

For each study, the proposal should be able to answer:

> **What does this discipline contribute that another discipline would not provide?**

This will help address the SENSS concern that the project needs more detail about the different disciplines and how they are integrated.

## Current Working Principle

The study architecture is not yet the final methodology.

The next stage is to test and refine:

1. participant groups;
2. recruitment routes;
3. geographical scope;
4. sample sizes;
5. specific methods;
6. analysis;
7. ethical safeguards;
8. study duration;
9. links between studies.

Only after these points have been tested should the detailed methodology and doctoral timeline be rewritten.

The proposal text should then be revised using the agreed principle:

> **Keep → Refine → Replace only where necessary.**

The objective is to make the existing proposal more focused, feasible and clearly interdisciplinary without unnecessarily discarding its original research foundation.
