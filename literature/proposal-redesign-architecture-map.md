# SENSS Proposal Redesign — Research Architecture Map

## Purpose

This document maps the original SENSS proposal against the feedback received from the SENSS Thematic Review Panel.

The purpose is **not to create a completely new research project**. The original proposal remains the foundation. The aim is to retain the strongest parts of the original submission while refining the research architecture, strengthening feasibility, and making the integration of the different disciplinary perspectives much clearer.

### SENSS feedback to address

> “The Panel felt that this is an ambitious project addressing a clear research problem. It is well-structured and has a clear methodology. However, the Panel also felt that the feasibility of the project needs more consideration due its ambitious nature. It would also benefit from more detail around the different disciplines and how they are integrated.”

The two main redesign priorities are therefore:

1. **Feasibility** — demonstrate that the project can realistically be completed within the available MPhil-PhD period.
2. **Interdisciplinary integration** — clearly explain what each disciplinary perspective contributes and how the perspectives work together within the research.

# 3. Emerging Logic of the Four Studies

The redesigned studies should form a connected programme rather than four largely separate projects.

## Study 1 — Justice responses after CGV

### Original role

The original Study 1 compared victims who had experienced:

- no justice;
- restorative justice; or
- punitive justice.

It examined PTSD symptoms, trust, attitudes towards offenders and perceived social cohesion.

### Redesign direction

Study 1 should be redesigned around an **evolutionary justice framework**.

The focus should move towards understanding the psychological conditions associated with preferences for:

- punishment;
- deterrence;
- compensation;
- repair;
- revenge; and/or
- reconciliation.

The study should retain the original concern with justice after CGV but avoid creating an unnecessarily difficult three-group comparison if this cannot be realistically recruited or interpreted.

### Contribution to the overall PhD

Study 1 establishes the psychological and evolutionary basis for understanding **what people want justice to achieve after CGV**.

---

## Study 2 — Offending, repair and reintegration

### Original role

The original Study 2 compared three categories of former offenders and examined:

- moral repair;
- social acceptance;
- trust;
- motivation to reintegrate; and
- post-intervention trajectories.

### Main feasibility concern

The original design requires access to several very specific categories of former offenders and makes ambitious claims about trajectories and recidivism.

This creates substantial recruitment, ethical and methodological challenges.

### Redesign direction

Study 2 should be redesigned around **evolutionary psychology and forensic psychology**.

It should examine how people connected to offending understand:

- punishment;
- repair;
- acceptance;
- reintegration;
- disengagement from violence; and
- continued relationships with the community.

Forensic psychology should make a genuine contribution to the study rather than simply appearing as an additional label.

### Contribution to the overall PhD

Study 2 extends the justice question from the perspective of offending and reintegration and connects the evolutionary account of justice with forensic perspectives on violence and reintegration.

---

## Study 3 — Community justice, reconciliation and local responses

### Original role

The original Study 3 followed participants before and after restorative justice interventions carried out by PIND.

It examined:

- justice needs;
- whether those needs were met;
- which parts of the intervention worked;
- what remained unresolved; and
- views of justice and social relationships after the intervention.

### Redesign direction

Study 3 should be **retained but broadened**.

The study can provide the main community perspective within the PhD.

Potential areas include:

- community understandings of justice;
- reconciliation;
- trust;
- social relationships;
- community norms;
- local responses to violence;
- community dialogue;
- traditional/community institutions; and
- experiences of justice or peacebuilding interventions.

A comparison between communities with **lower and higher levels of CGV exposure** may also be considered, provided that this can be justified and carried out feasibly.

Participant selection must be carefully considered. If community leaders are included, the proposal should explain why their perspective is important and what perspectives may not be captured by focusing on leaders.

### Contribution to the overall PhD

Study 3 connects the individual justice perspectives examined earlier with the wider community context in which justice, reconciliation and reintegration take place.

---

## Study 4 — Integrating justice priorities

### Original role

The original Study 4 adapted behavioural process-tracing to examine which justice needs people prioritise when judging whether justice has been achieved.

Participants would select questions linked to justice needs identified in Study 3.

### Redesign direction

Study 4 should remain, but its role should become clearer.

Rather than appearing as an independent experiment, it should **use the justice needs and priorities identified in the preceding studies**.

The study can examine:

- which justice needs people seek information about;
- which needs they prioritise;
- whether priorities differ according to previous CGV experience; and
- whether priorities differ between victims/community members/offender-connected participants or other clearly justified groups.

### Contribution to the overall PhD

Study 4 becomes the **integration stage** of the research.

It takes findings from the earlier studies and examines how people use those justice considerations when deciding whether justice has actually been achieved.

# 4. Emerging Interdisciplinary Structure

The disciplines should not be presented as separate subjects that happen to appear in the same PhD.

They should work together.

## Evolutionary psychology

Contributes to understanding:

- punishment;
- deterrence;
- revenge;
- compensation;
- repair;
- reconciliation; and
- the psychological conditions underlying justice preferences.

## Forensic psychology

Contributes to understanding:

- violence;
- offending;
- disengagement from violence;
- reintegration;
- offender perspectives;
- risk-related considerations;
- sensitive populations; and
- ethical research involving people connected to violence.

## Community and peacebuilding perspectives

Contribute to understanding:

- local concepts of justice;
- community norms;
- community dialogue;
- traditional/community institutions;
- trust;
- reconciliation;
- social cohesion; and
- local responses to violence.

## Integration

Together, these perspectives should help answer the broader question:

> **How is justice understood, chosen and experienced after cult and gang-related violence, and how do different justice responses relate to repair, reintegration, trust and social cohesion?**

The final proposal should explicitly show **where these disciplinary perspectives interact within the studies**, rather than simply listing them in the background section.

# 5. Key Feasibility Questions to Resolve Before Rewriting

Before the final proposal is rewritten, the following issues need to be resolved.

### Recruitment

- Who exactly will participate in each study?
- How will participants be accessed?
- What role will AAPW and PIND play?
- Can the required sample or participant group realistically be recruited?

### Geographic scope

- Which Southern Nigerian locations are genuinely necessary?
- Can the geographical scope be narrowed without weakening the research?
- Would comparing selected communities be more feasible than covering multiple states?

### Participant groups

- Do we need victims, offenders, community members and leaders in every study?
- Which participant groups are essential for each research question?
- Can the number of groups be reduced?

### Ethics

- How will retraumatisation be minimised?
- How will confidentiality be protected?
- How will researcher safety be addressed?
- How will the researcher's insider position be managed?
- What justification is needed for involving people connected to offending?

### Methods

- Which methods are essential?
- Which methods can be simplified?
- Which studies genuinely need quantitative methods?
- Which questions are better answered qualitatively?
- Which elements need longitudinal data?

### Timeline

- Can all four studies realistically fit within 54 months?
- Which activities can overlap?
- Which study must happen before another?
- How much time is realistically required for UK and Nigerian ethics?
- How much time is required for recruitment, fieldwork, analysis and writing?

# 6. Working Principle for the Redesign

The proposal should follow:

> **Keep → Refine → Replace only where necessary.**

### Keep

Strong parts of the original proposal that remain relevant and defensible.

### Refine

Parts that are good but need clearer links to the revised research architecture.

### Replace

Only those elements that create unnecessary feasibility problems or no longer fit the redesigned research questions.

The final proposal should therefore remain recognisably the same research project while being:

- more coherent;
- more feasible;
- more clearly interdisciplinary;
- more methodologically defensible; and
- more clearly connected from Study 1 through Study 4.
